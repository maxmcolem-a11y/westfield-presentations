03 / 15
Origami Wagon | August Product Story

SKW-033

One sheet replaces the frame, bag, and fasteners.

  • The category problem: folding wagons still use the same tube-frame and fabric-bag architecture, leaving the category to compete on features and price.
  • The product idea: score one polymer sheet so the basket carries the load and performs the fold; the creases become the hinges.
  • The proof shown: a solved one-degree-of-freedom mechanism with 26 rigid panels, 78 creases, and a cut-ready tooling file.
  • The payoff: walls-only ribs carry the structure while the body targets a 50 kg payload and a roughly four-inch collapsed bundle.

Instant Indoor | August Product Story

SKW-041

A sofa platform built around parcel-ready deployment.

  • The category problem: furniture solved delivery with oversized freight or by making the customer finish the product.
  • The product idea: use Westfield's folding steel-tube expertise to create a quad-fold frame that opens without tools.
  • The platform: one 42 × 42 inch frame becomes an armchair, armless chair, corner, ottoman, sofa, or sectional.
  • The proof shown: the finished armchair sample—the hardest configuration—was beside the screen for people to sit in and inspect.

Diamond + Spiral | August Product Story

TB4695 + TB4696

Make the volume real before drawing the soft goods.

  • The process: twenty volumetric solids were modeled, three were printed at 1:5 scale, and only two advanced to samples.
  • Diamond: a structured 20-liter body built from mirrored triangular panels with a slanted front access zipper.
  • Spiral: a 22-liter sling-and-pack hybrid whose front panel wraps into the hip so pack and belt read as one form.
  • The proof shown: first samples changed pockets, mesh, and webbing—but not the silhouettes chosen from the physical form studies.

GRAM Chair

A rocking chair with no rocker.

09 / 15
GRAM Chair | August Product Story

Innovation S28

The frame becomes the motion mechanism.

  • The category problem: portable rockers bolt runners, springs, or linkages onto a frame that already has to fold.
  • The product idea: replace eight rigid elbows with elastomeric corners that act as the pivot and the return spring.
  • The tunable feel: wall thickness, flexure length, and durometer set the rocking response without changing the frame.
  • The proof shown: working samples in the room let visitors feel the motion, inspect underneath, and see that nothing was added below the seat.

Suspend Chair | August Product Story

SKW-044

Two frames: one hangs inside the other.

  • The category problem: most camp chairs either fold or move; motion usually arrives as hardware added beneath the seat.
  • The product idea: suspend the inner seat frame from the arms on four static webbing straps so it swings and pitches inside the outer frame.
  • The experience: the seat travels forward and back along an arc, rises at full travel, and returns under gravity rather than a spring.
  • The proof shown: the sample delivered 5.6 inches of travel while the suspension remained entirely above the seat.

Hawkeye V3 | August Product Story

SKW-018

The runner did not disappear. It became the spring.

  • The category problem: rail rockers, hinged rockers, and plungers each constrain the user to one motion axis.
  • The V2 lesson: a spring behind the rider returned a short shudder; moving the spring under the rider changed the experience.
  • The product idea: two arched bars create four live responses—bounce, rock, roll, and twist—with no coils, pivots, or hinges.
  • The production direction: steel proved the geometry; a tuned composite bar cuts weight and makes the layup the tuning tool.
