Instant Indoor | Updates Since August

Frame architecture revision

The next sample is simpler to deploy—and closer to indoor furniture.

Continuous risers

Four telescoping corner poles replace the split upright approach, reducing parts and setup steps.

Stronger frame

Metal wall thickness moves to 1.4 mm; the X geometry and its covering supports are widened to improve folding behavior.

Real suspension

Five GOODSLAT supports are sewn into the outer cover and land on the frame so comfort is not dependent on fabric tension alone.

Soft-goods system

Foam layers and slipcover attachment logic are modeled; the larger center volume may also store detachable soft parts.

GRAM Chair | Product Mechanism

Nothing added under the seat

The frame becomes the rocking mechanism.

Eight rigid elbows are replaced by elastomeric corners. The square frame shears into a parallelogram; each corner pivots and returns.

No runners. No springs. No linkages. The familiar folding architecture stays intact.
8Compliant corners
0Added linkages
3Tuning levers
What changes

The corner geometry and elastomer specification.

What stays

The tube frame, fold, pack size, and recognizable chair architecture.

GRAM Chair | Tuning + Next Generation

Make the feel measurable

A narrow window separates a rocker from a dead chair.

Target the viable band

Below 120 N·m/rad the chair falls over; above 300 it feels stable but dead. The current recommendation is 240.

One molding per side

A rigid structural body and soft elastomer corners become one two-shot side frame.

Two frames. One hangs.

Hawkeye V3 | The Lesson from V2

Not quite it

The spring was behind the rider.

V2 proved the response was possible, but the rear spring limb only traveled about a finger's width. The rider felt a quick shudder—not the long, controlled motion the product needs.

Move the spring under the rider, where body weight can load the full arch.

FlowMotion V2 | Product Mechanism

One spring. Two different jobs.

The wedge turns the leaf spring into a tunable system.

V1 proved the flat leaf spring could move, but its symmetric response offered one fixed feel. V2 angles the spring to create a fixed rear fulcrum and a tunable forward fulcrum.

The same frame and spring can now be tuned for resistance, travel, return, and pinch clearance through one wedge.
2Functional fulcrums
1Tuning variable
0Frame redesigns
Rear fulcrum

A shorter fixed lever arm makes the initial lean-back easy and natural.

Forward fulcrum

Its position sets resistance, motion amplitude, and the pinch-safe gap.
