AI Basics

A Simple Explanation: What Is an LLM?

A Large Language Model is an AI model trained to work with language: reading, summarizing, comparing, drafting, answering questions, and creating structured outputs.

Examples

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are AI assistants built on large language models. Llama is a family of large language models. The LLM is the language engine inside the broader AI system.

What they are good at

  • Reading large amounts of text quickly
  • Summarizing documents
  • Finding patterns across notes
  • Drafting emails, reports, briefs, and presentations
  • Translating messy inputs into structured outputs
  • Explaining complex information simply

AI Basics

AI Basics

A Simple Explanation: What Is an AI Agent?

An AI agent is an AI system that has a role, access to approved information, and the ability to perform a workflow repeatedly.

Reactive

Chatbot

You ask a question. It answers.

Workflow-based

Agent

It has a job. It checks information, prepares outputs, watches for changes, and supports a workflow.

Example

A Product Management Agent could review Trello every morning, identify late sample risks, summarize upcoming line review needs, and draft action items for the PM team.

AI Basics

AI Basics

What Is an Agentic System?

An agentic system is a set of AI agents that can perform defined jobs, use approved information, and coordinate steps inside a business process.

What it is

Multiple task-focused agents working from shared data, tools, rules, and human oversight.

How companies use it

Monitoring workflows, summarizing changes, preparing drafts, flagging risks, routing work, and keeping teams aligned.

Why it matters

The system turns repeated information work into a more consistent operating rhythm without removing human judgment.

The key difference: a chatbot answers a request; an agentic system supports an ongoing workflow.

Trello Source of Truth

Trello Primer

What Trello Is and Why It Matters to AI Agents

Trello is a Kanban-based project management system: work is organized into boards, lists, and cards that can hold owners, dates, labels, comments, checklists, attachments, and decisions.

Kanban-based work system

Boards show the workflow, lists show stages, and cards carry the actual work item from idea to decision to completion.

Single source of truth

When the card is maintained well, the latest status, context, files, comments, owners, and open questions live in one place instead of scattered across messages and memory.

Structured enough for agents

Trello gives agents clean objects to read: boards, cards, labels, due dates, members, checklist items, comments, attachments, and activity history.

Direct API access

Agents can safely read and update approved boards through Trello's API, summarize changes, flag missing information, draft reports, and keep cards current.

Part of a broader category

Trello sits in a wider species of work-management tools alongside platforms like Asana, Monday, Jira, ClickUp, Smartsheet, Airtable, and Microsoft Planner.

Not locked to one vendor

The important idea is structured work: projects, owners, dates, comments, attachments, decisions, and status history organized so people and agents can use them.

Trello is the current practical foundation, not the only possible answer. The strategic requirement is a structured work system agents can read, summarize, update, and turn into useful visibility.
