Product Thesis

Cool the person, not the environment.

Fixed air conditioning cools buildings. Fans lose effectiveness in high heat and humidity. Evaporative cooling fails when wet-bulb conditions make sweat evaporation unreliable.

Earth Suit is personal climate gear: a mobile thermal system that removes body heat directly from the highest-value cooling zones.

Architecture

Backpack refrigeration, garment heat pickup.

Backpack cooling plant

Do the heavy thermal work off-body.

  • Condenser and high-flow exhaust fan
  • Liquid chiller heat exchanger
  • Pump, battery, control electronics
  • Condensate and safety sensor package

Wearable cooling layer

Move heat out of the body through liquid.

  • Washable vest shell
  • Removable tube network
  • Quick-disconnect ports at lower back
  • Insulated coolant lines
  • Upper back, chest, neck, underarm zones
No ducts. Liquid is the right transfer medium.

Why Compressor

This is the unlock.

Thermoelectric modules can create a cool touch, but they do not efficiently remove working-human heat loads. Vapor compression moves heat with a real refrigeration cycle, so the system can deliver useful BTUs from a battery-sized package.

Technology Choice

Why not Peltier, piezo, or ducted air?

Peltier / TEC

Battery and heat rejection collapse the idea.

Low efficiency means the system must dump body heat plus substantial electrical waste heat. Heat sinks, fans, and battery mass grow fast.

Fan / evaporative

Useful comfort, weak wet-bulb solution.

Airflow and mist feel good in moderate conditions, but they rely on evaporation and ambient air that may already be hostile.

Ducted air

Too bulky and leaky for workwear.

Ducts add volume, noise, routing issues, and poor sealing under motion. Liquid carries more heat in less space.

Peltier can be a small localized feature later, not the main thermal engine.

Market Landscape

The market is trying, but most products stop short of real refrigeration.

Ice water loop

AlphaCool, Gerbing, TechNiche, Compcooler

Real liquid transfer, but depends on frozen water or ice. Strong for short sessions, weak as a professional all-day platform.

Fan / mist / plate

Shark ChillPill, Sony REON, fan vests

Convenient consumer cooling, but not designed to remove active-worker BTU loads from the torso.

Compressor claims

CoolingTechFactory, Coolwair

Closest technical direction. Current visibility suggests early or narrow products, not a broad outdoor/work platform yet.

Product Direction

Small backpack, serious field language.

The backpack should read closer to a compact hydration or battery pack than a camping backpack. The visual target is rugged, serviceable, and credible, with visible condenser exhaust treated as a performance feature.

Keep

Compact vertical chassis, shoulder harness integration, lower-back coolant ports, clear battery status, and durable exterior.

Avoid

Sci-fi armor, bulky ducting, oversized pack volume, hidden airflow, and lifestyle-only cooling claims.

The first product is a vest/backpack platform. The full suit comes after proof.

Critical Path

Kickoff steps to make this real.

01

Compressor facts

Confirm exact model, angle tolerance, refrigerant, capacity curve, input power, duty cycle, and supplier support.

02

Thermal loop

Build benchtop compressor, condenser, chiller, pump, reservoir, and simulated human heat load.

03

BTU validation

Measure actual heat removal, battery draw, condenser temperature, noise, and orientation sensitivity.

04

Vest layout

Prototype removable tube network across upper back, chest, neck, and underarms.

05

Backpack ID

Package compressor, fan path, battery, controls, service access, harness, and coolant ports.

06

User test

Run hot-room or outdoor trials against fan, ice bladder, and passive cooling references.

07

Sample brief

Lock V1 target specs, BOM direction, supplier brief, and safety language for first professional sample.

Decision gate: prove BTUs and wearable comfort before expensive tooling.

Honest Compromises

The constraints are manageable if we design around them early.

The refrigeration path is the only path with enough useful capacity, but it creates real product work. These should be framed as engineering gates, not hidden issues.

Orientation

Broadly upright operation

Backpack behavior needs defined operating angles and shutdown logic.

Airflow

Condenser exhaust is mandatory

The pack must reject more heat than the vest removes.

Condensation

Moisture must be controlled

Cold surfaces, tubing, and vest comfort need drain or insulation strategy.

Battery

Runtime drives weight and cost

Swappable battery architecture should be part of the first sample plan.

The upside is real BTU capacity. That is the product.

Recommendation

Proceed to thermal proof.

Build the benchtop loop first, then develop the vest and backpack around measured cooling performance. If the system proves 900-1,100 BTU/hr in a wearable package, Earth Suit becomes a credible new platform for extreme-heat adaptation.
