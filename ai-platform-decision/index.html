52.4 tok/s is fleet demand averaged over the assumed service window—not a user’s streaming speed. The retained usage data does not include per-call generation latency. The 35.75 tok/s figure comes from the vendor’s one-request test. Three machines do not make an individual answer generate at three times that speed.

The scaling retains automation in these six accounts, but excludes the primary administrator, other employees, separate image generation and CAD/tool compute. Background work is compressed into the 176-hour window; moving eligible jobs overnight could flatten demand.

Busy days and cache sensitivity—the limits that change the answer

October 13 was the largest daily output bucket: 948,083 output tokens for the six accounts. At five times that activity, the same local share and an eight-hour window, demand becomes 146.8 output tok/s + 1,028 fresh input tok/s. That is 4.44 continuously busy Sparks: about 148% of three units or 111% of four, before a reserve. The same 70%-load rule would round to seven for that full day.

This is a busy-day sensitivity, not a recommendation to buy seven. Each turn/model aggregate is assigned to its first-call date; none of the included blocks crossed midnight. Intraday arrival and concurrency peaks are unknown. Queuing, overnight scheduling and cloud overflow may be preferable to buying for occasional peaks. Even a sustained 2× average load takes three to 110% and four to 82%.

Without reuse, the input demand alone becomes 10,260 tok/s. Cache reuse must be measured on the selected model/software, with memory limits, eviction, restarts and session-to-replica routing included. vLLM prefix caching avoids repeated context processing; it does not eliminate output generation.

Deployment assumptions, validation and reproducible calculations

Run independent Qwen replicas behind a queue and load balancer on ordinary networking, with session affinity to help preserve context reuse. Each Spark has 128 GB shared memory; FP8 weights for a 35B model are roughly 35 GB before caches and runtime overhead. The vendor has demonstrated this model on one Spark, but thirty users’ simultaneous contexts still require testing. The original servers continue running agents and records.

Native model context is 262,144 tokens; the cited benchmark tests 128K. Some existing cloud calls exceed both. Route oversized jobs to cloud models or validate compaction—do not silently discard context. The rates are published rounded phase rates; prefill plus decode arithmetic does not reproduce all 73 seconds of vendor end-to-end latency. We assume neither concurrency speedups nor Qwen parity on our workflows.

Validation gate: replay representative low/medium jobs on one Spark; verify useful outputs, FP8/tool compatibility, real token counts, cache hit rate, memory, time to first token and total completion. Replay recorded busy-day volumes at 1, 4, 8 and 16 simultaneous requests. Exercise restart, failed replica and cloud overflow before fixing fleet size.

Three machines add a planning power allowance of up to 720 W; four, 960 W. Drivers, serving software, security, model versions, backups, queues and recovery stay ours. Offloaded API usage must be removed from the budget so we do not charge twice for the same work.

06 / Recommended next step

Evaluate the alternatives.
Then commit to the rollout.

A proposed four-week comparison—not a migration approval.

  1. Keep the lab working.

    Preserve current users and workflows. The pilot is the reference standard, not a system to discard.

  2. Test Grok on the hard jobs.

    Measure accepted output, employee correction time and human support. Test failed runs and recovery, not only successful demos.

  3. Price the full obligation.

    Get a refreshed hardware/build/support proposal and a vendor proposal covering usage, controls and response commitments.

Replacement requirements

  • Full Trello execution
  • Editable Office files
  • CAD & color fidelity
  • Email & file transfer
  • Reliable background work
  • Permissions & recovery

One platform must earn the decision.

Choose OpenClaw if its control and working methods justify owning the full system.

Choose Grok or another commercial service if it preserves that work with less friction and human support.

Why “supports Trello” is not enough

Our replacement must upload and download real files, preserve covers and attachments, handle comments, checklists and custom fields, and update linked cards without duplicates. The standard connector is incomplete; the fuller API offers a route, not proven end-to-end parity. Connector scope · Trello API

Both platforms must verify delivery and stop completed jobs. The lab’s completed-task controller has been disabled; Grok also documents routine-recovery limits. Vendor hosting does not remove workflow maintenance.

Calculation and limits

99 active user-days × assumed minutes saved ÷ 60 = 24.75, 49.5 or 99 hours. Multiplying by five gives 123.75, 247.5 or 495 hours for 30 equally active users. Values above are rounded. Active dates may include follow-ups or personal interactions; they are not full business-workdays.

Net saving here means after prompting, review, correction and rework—not after shared administration. No evidence establishes which case is most likely.
