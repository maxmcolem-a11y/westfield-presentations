Westfield Skunk Works · SKW-018

It doesn't just rock.

A rocker runs you along one arc, at one rate. Hawkeye sits on two arched spring bars that give in four directions at once — bounce, rock, roll and twist — so the chair answers whatever you do instead of asking you to steer it.

Two formed bars · no coil spring · no pivot · no hinge

01 — What everything else does

Every motion chair moves on exactly one axis.

01 · RAILS

FlowMotion skis and GCI runners roll you fore and aft along a fixed arc. Good motion — but it is one motion.

02 · HINGES

Freestyle-style spring rockers put a pivot under the seat. A hinge has one degree of freedom by definition.

03 · PLUNGERS

Our own DC-0024 bounces straight up and down on a steel spring, and only up and down.

Sit still and they are all the same chair. Move, and they all put you back on their one path.

02 — What we actually changed

The runner didn't go away. It became the spring.

There is still a member on the ground front to rear — it is an arch now, not a ski, and it is the only springy part in the chair. One part holds you up, sets the ride and does the folding, so the motion stopped being something we bolt on.

50.8 mm strip · R 800 arc · 648.5 mm between pads · four half-beams doing all of the work

03 — The first answer

V2 put the spring behind you.

A shaped composite limb under the rear foot — properly made, and the sample felt solid in the hand. But the spring sat behind the person rather than under them, so what came back was a short, quick shudder instead of a ride.

04 — What it taught us

It isn't the spring. It's where the spring sits.

01 · BEHIND YOU

A spring behind the rear foot has to lift you over it before anything moves, and what little travel there is arrives at the back of the seat.

02 · UNDER YOU

Four springs at the corners of the base take your weight where you actually sit, so the give arrives underneath you.

03 · THE DIFFERENCE

Same idea and much the same materials. One you have to drive. The other one answers.

V2 was a good chair with its spring in the wrong place — and finding that out is what pointed at the arch.

05 — The move that worked

Stop springing the leg. Spring the base.

Two arched bars, one each side, each carrying you on two half-beams. Four springs at the four corners of the chair — so weight anywhere on the seat finds a different combination of them, and the chair leans, dips or turns to match.

06 — What you actually feel

Four ways to move, all live at once.

Bounce · 1.8 Hz

Both arches flatten together. The quick give when you drop into it or push with your heels.

Rock · 1.3 Hz

The arches trade height front to rear. The long one — half the rate of the others, so it leads.

Roll · 1.8 Hz

One side gives, the other lifts. Lean on an arm and the chair leans with you.

Twist · soft

A 50.8 mm bar has almost no torsional stiffness, so the seat winds slightly as you turn.

You get the one you ask for. Lean back and it rocks; drop into it and it gives; lean on an arm and it follows.

07 — Why that is more fun

A slow rock, on a quick spring.

The rock comes out at 1.3 Hz and the give and the lean at about 1.8 — so you get a long rocking stroke with a lively spring underneath it, not one rate for everything. A rail rocker has a single rate and a single path: whatever you do, it hands you the same stroke back.

YOU DON'T DRIVE IT

A rocker has to be pumped along its arc. This answers a shrug.

IT STAYS PUT

A rail rocker walks its contact 140 mm across the ground. Four pads don't move at all.

NOTHING TO LEARN

No arc to find and no rhythm to catch — it answers the first thing you do in it.

08 — What it is made of

Steel proved it. Composite ships it.

The sample bars are spring steel because steel is quick to form and quick to argue with. The production bar is composite — same stiffness at 7.2 mm, half the weight, no plating and no corrosion. And the tune stops being a thickness and becomes a layup.

09 — The next sample

How deep the arch has to be, solved.

The bar carries you in bending, so the vertical mode obeys the same law that beat V2: f = (1/2π)√(g/δ). Want 1.8 Hz, and you have just asked for 77 mm of sag. Add the travel you want left over and the rise falls out — 170 mm, R 394, against today's 45.

Today's arch is shallower than its own sag — it flattens under the occupant before it can move
