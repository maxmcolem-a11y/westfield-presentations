What the fold is doing

The body is a concertina bellows, not a flat accordion stack: 12 pleat rows on a fixed slant length L = 38 mm, with diagonal creases running across every face so each pleat is a shallow V that nests into its neighbors and interlocks at the corners. The fold angle φ is still the single degree of freedom — height is 12·L·cos φ, pleat depth is L·sin φ — and nothing stretches, which is what makes a welded construction possible. Because the crease pattern is fixed in the flat sheet, the V-drop scales with pleat pitch: the diagonals are pronounced when expanded and flatten as the body packs down.

Those diagonals are the difference between a concertina and a plain accordion, and they're structural, not styling: they triangulate the large facets so they can't oil-can, they let the corners fold without bunching (the reason camera bellows are cut this way), and they bias every pleat to collapse in the same sequence instead of buckling randomly. The rim inherits the last pleat's V — which is why the liner collar shows through at the face centers, exactly as in the prototype renders — and the lid architecture has to own that.

Note the inversion: pleats stick out further when compressed (depth 17 → 37 mm across the range). The compressed footprint grows ~40 mm in width and depth — straps and any carry frame must clear that.

Answer to Nessa's "does it fold flat?": not flat — it compresses to roughly 1/3 height (≈410 → ≈120 mm body) plus a fixed base and lid stack. Locked states, not a flat-pack.

Material stack — recommended

The move that makes this manufacturable: the foam facet is both the structure and the insulation. Rigid panels aren't a separate skeleton — die-cut closed-cell foam pads sit in welded pockets between two weldable skins, and the bare laminate between pads is the hinge. One material system, one factory type.

Outer skin — 840D nylon, TPU-laminated both sidesweldable, abrasion + UV package, premium hand; this is standard welded-cooler shell material

0.5 mm

Base: molded EVA/PU tray, welded to the shell. Hardware: acetal/PP cam buckles + ladderlocks on bartacked webbing, anchored on ridge weld-lands. Expect soft-cooler-class retention (~24 h ice), not hard-cooler — hinge lines cap the ceiling; the labyrinth offset and hinge strips are what keep it near that number.

Insulation physics to hold onto: heat takes the parallel path. Keep hinge area under ~12% of total wall, never let inner and outer hinge lines stack on the same vertical plane, and the pleats cost you surprisingly little. Let them stack, and the cooler grows cold-leak stripes you can see on a thermal camera.

How to actually make it

The unlock: the pleat pattern is developable — it unrolls flat. So you never manufacture a folded thing. You cut flat, weld flat on a flatbed RF machine (the weld bars double as crease setters), then the first compression folds the body into shape. That is standard soft-goods flow plus weld fixtures, not exotic tooling.

  1. Prove the motion — 3D print (the August sample)Print TPU 95A, single wall 1.2–1.6 mm with hinge grooves thinned to 0.6–0.8 mm, pleats oriented vertically on the bed. It proves fold sequence, lock states, and rim behavior — not materials or thermal.
  2. Flat-pattern soft prototypeCNC/laser-cut the laminate blanks and die-cut foam facets; bond foam, weld the two side seams, sew the collar, bolt on bought hardware. One week of sample-room time per iteration.
  3. Coupon tests before toolingRun the hinge, not the cooler: 10k fold cycles, −20 °C cold-crack fold, weld peel, puncture, stain/cleanability, UV. Kill bad stacks at $50 a coupon, not $5k a tool.
  4. Production cut & weldDie-cut laminate + foam → flatbed RF/HF weld: pocket welds capture each facet, crease welds set each hinge → two vertical body seams close the tube.
  5. Liner and baseThermoform or RF-weld the TPU bucket liner; weld its rim to the shell collar and its floor to the base tray. Interior stays a single smooth surface.
  6. Hardware & carryBartack webbing onto welded anchor patches on ridge lands; cam buckles set the locked volume states; backpack harness anchors to base tray and rim, not to pleats.
  7. QC every unitAir-pressurize ~0.2–0.3 psi and dunk for leaks; 20 fold cycles; rim-fit gauge at each lock state.

Factory profile: a welded soft-cooler / dry-bag factory with flatbed RF welders — the same supplier pool as the current welded-cooler line and, likely, Origami Wagon. Tooling is cutting dies + weld fixtures + one thermoform tool for the liner: light, and per-size cost stays low, which protects the "scalable architecture" goal on the card.

What the August print must prove (gate to spending on soft tooling):

  • Repeatable fold sequence — 20 cycles, no pleat skipping, by hand
  • Stands upright loaded at full, mid, and compressed states
  • Rim stays plan-flat within ~3 mm across all lock states
  • No pinch points on the liner path when compressing
  • Strap take-up per lock state is a length a buckle can actually manage
