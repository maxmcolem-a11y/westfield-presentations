Concept 01 · Rev F — load-opposed fold, collision-swept

Flat-Fold Cantilever Chair

Every joint folds against its use load — sitting, leaning back, or pushing up on the arms presses each member into a hard stop, and no latch ever carries a person. The big around-rotations demand clear sweep corridors, so the members run in offset lanes: rails 28 in, runners 28 out — flush against the legs — backs 56 out, arms 84 out. The whole fold is numerically swept for member-to-member collisions.

Joint logic — load vs. fold

  • SHOULDER ×2Recline pushes the back rearward into its stop. Fold goes forward, +107°, the back lying down beside the arm in the next lane out.
  • WRIST ×2Pushing up out of the chair loads the armrest down into its stop. Fold swings arm + back up and over the front, +289° — the outboard lane keeps the whole sweep clear of the X and the drop.
  • SEAT KNUCKLE ×2Sitting drives the cantilevered rail rear-end-down into its stop — the big joint carries this on a stop face, not a latch. Fold sweeps the rail up and over, +284°, resting inboard beside the drop.
  • RUNNER ×2Ground reaction pushes the foot up into its stop. Fold rotates down and around, +300°, nesting flush along the leg — just one tube-width out, on a compact boss at the joint.
  • SPREADER XTwo chains meet at the center boss — one pinned between the knuckles, one between lower drop pins — so the open X reads true. Seat-fabric tension jams each elbow onto a fixed stop plate; no sitter load can move them. To fold, the center boss swivels half a turn through the front, then the chains scissor apart inline with the leg plane — one elbow up the plane, one down, links ending parallel to the drops inside the pack.

Packed: a flat bar ≈ 720 × 290 × 140 mm, everything parallel to the drop line. The fold is swept end-to-end in software: minimum clearance between any two members stays positive through all five stages — the offsets and the 4–20° splay of each member's resting angle are what buy that. Boss widths at the wrist, knuckle, and runner joints absorb the lane jogs.

⟳ Spring deploy is a curiosity, not the baseline. Because every fold is load-opposed and rotational, deploy-biased springs push the same way the sitter's weight does — onto the stops. The button shows the cascade version: width blooms open, then each joint trips the next in reverse fold order, rebounding softly off its stop. V1 keeps springs for parking only; the cascade's trip linkages and a damped arm swing are a v2 conversation.
