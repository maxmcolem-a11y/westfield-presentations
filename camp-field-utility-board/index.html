Camp / Field Utility Board

A focused product case for turning ironing-board mechanics into a fast-deploying outdoor work surface.

Westfield Outdoors · Camp / Field Utility Board
01 / 09

Trend Signal

A strange behavior showed us a useful product logic.

People in Belgium have been carrying ironing boards into parks, street parties, student gatherings, and raves, then using them as shared outdoor tables.

We are not treating that as validated demand. We are treating it as early evidence that a fold-flat, height-adjustable surface already makes sense outside the laundry room.

  • The behavior is social-media-documented and repeatedly visible enough to study.
  • The table use is practical: drinks, food, phones, and shared group surface area.
  • The real insight is the mechanism, not the novelty of the object.
02 / 09

Westfield Plan

Translate the hack into a simple outdoor utility platform.

The plan is not to build a better party ironing board. It is to turn the behavior into one clear Westfield product: a fast-deploying, height-adjustable work surface for camp and field use.

Product focus

One portable outdoor board that gives users instant, adjustable work surface.

Use cases

Camp prep, fishing, hunting, gardening, tailgating, range work, and gear staging.

Mechanism proof

Validate fast deployment, infinite height adjustment, positive lock, and loaded stability.

Outdoor fit

Hard top, oversized feet, carry handle, simple packout, and field-cleanable surfaces.

Cost logic

Keep the frame simple: tube structure, basic hinges, one-touch handle, and minimal springs.

Prototype goal

Build the simplest credible version and test whether the user behavior feels immediate.

03 / 09

Deployment

Walk it over. Pop the legs out. It is ready.

An ironing board has a rare setup behavior: one person carries it to the spot, swings the legs open with a quick arm motion, and immediately has a usable surface.

SecondsThe value is instant setup, not a table that asks the user to assemble parts.

Mechanism Anatomy

What the mechanism gets right.

The reason this is worth studying is that the base architecture already solves a lot of portable-surface problems before we redesign anything.

Where it earns its keep

Folds flat for storage and transport

Fast to set up and break down

Multiple height positions

Simple, low-cost metal tube construction

Familiar lock architecture

05 / 09

Outdoor Failure Points

Where outdoor use exposes it.

The design challenge is keeping the useful simplicity while correcting the risks that show up once the board leaves flat indoor flooring.

What the Camp / Field version must solve

Wobble and tip risk under real outdoor loads

Indoor surface is not field-durable

Small feet that sink in soft ground

Accidental collapse risk

Narrow stance on rough ground

06 / 09

Outdoor Translation

Keep the mechanism. Rebuild the surface and feet for the field.

The concept should not inherit the weak parts of a household ironing board. It should preserve the deployment logic while correcting the outdoor failure points.

  • Rigid wipeable or perforated hard top
  • Oversized feet for grass, gravel, dirt, and sand
  • Positive lock feedback under load
  • Carry handle, wagon storage, and tie-down logic
07 / 09

MVP Path

Critical path to an August low-cost working sample.

Factory visit · nowChoose the donor frame

Find the closest existing ironing-board frame: cheapest, strongest, widest stance, and easiest to modify. Capture folded size, height range, tube sizes, lock style, rivets, hinges, and current BOM.

This weekFreeze the MVP scope

One board only: existing folding frame, hard outdoor top, wider feet, usable carry/packout, and lock feedback. Exclude styling, product family ideas, and anything that triggers tooling before proof.

Mid-JuneDFM and quote check

Separate reuse from new work. Confirm what can be sampled from existing tube, hinge, lock, and assembly processes. Price the top, feet, labor, MOQ risk, and any unavoidable tooling.

Late JuneFirst rough prototype

Build ugly if needed. It only has to stand, fold, adjust, lock, and hold load. Test side load, rough ground, foot sinking, height positions, carry feel, and folded thickness.

JulyCorrect the weak points

Revise stance, top stiffness, foot geometry, lock feel, and packout. Decide whether the mechanism is strong enough for outdoor use before investing in cosmetic refinement.

Late July / AugustReview sample decision

Deliver 2-3 credible samples with target BOM, open issues, and go/no-go recommendation: continue into product development, simplify further, or stop if the mechanism cannot meet the use case.

02

05

Can the height range include table height and bar / gathering height?
