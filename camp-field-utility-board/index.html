Camp / Field Utility Board

A focused product case for turning ironing-board mechanics into a fast-deploying outdoor work surface.

Westfield Outdoors · Camp / Field Utility Board
01 / 10

Trend Signal

A strange behavior showed us a useful product logic.

People in Belgium have been carrying ironing boards into parks, street parties, student gatherings, and raves, then using them as shared outdoor tables.

We are not treating that as validated demand. We are treating it as early evidence that a fold-flat, height-adjustable surface already makes sense outside the laundry room.

  • The behavior is social-media-documented and repeatedly visible enough to study.
  • The table use is practical: drinks, food, phones, and shared group surface area.
  • The real insight is the mechanism, not the novelty of the object.
02 / 10

Westfield Plan

Translate the hack into a simple outdoor utility platform.

The plan is not to build a better party ironing board. It is to take the architecture seriously and move it into Westfield territory: outdoor furniture, camp utility, wagons, carts, coolers, and field work.

Lead concept

Camp / Field Utility Board as the first and cleanest product expression.

Mechanism proof

Validate fold-flat deployment, infinite height adjustment, locking, feet, and loaded stability.

Outdoor fit

Use a hard top, field-ready feet, carry logic, and wagon-compatible storage.

Business lanes

Camp, hunt, fish, garden, tailgate, range, coolers, drink support, and utility systems.

VA / VE path

Reuse tubing, hinges, locks, feet, handles, and table components where possible.

Future family

If the mechanism proves out, extend into wagon-side surfaces and related fold-flat table formats.

03 / 10

Deployment

Walk it over. Pop the legs out. It is ready.

An ironing board has a rare setup behavior: one person carries it to the spot, swings the legs open with a quick arm motion, and immediately has a usable surface.

01 Carry

Flat profile moves easily through a house, campsite, garage, dock, or tailgate area.

02 Swing

The leg system opens in one familiar motion instead of requiring assembly.

03 Lock

The same handle action puts the frame into a working position.

04 Use

The user gets instant counter space without building, unfolding, or clipping together a camp table.

04 / 10

Adjustment

Infinite height adjustment is the real unlock.

A simple one-touch lever lets the surface move to the user's body, task, terrain, and chair height. It is not locked into two or three preset positions.

The important behavior: it can be adjusted even with items already sitting on the table, because the user is lifting the surface rather than rebuilding the product.

Standing

Prep, grilling, fishing, range work, garden tasks.

Table / Desk

Camp meals, laptop work, drink station, gear staging.

Low

Coffee-table height for chairs, coolers, kids, or lounge use.

05 / 10

Simplicity

One spring can do both jobs.

The mechanism is valuable because it is not clever in an expensive way. The handle spring can support both adjustment and locking behavior.

That means fewer components, fewer failure points, and a better path to a durable outdoor version that still feels obvious to use.

06 / 10

Why This Works

It solves a real outdoor problem with a mechanism people already understand.

Camp and field users constantly need temporary surface area. Existing solutions are often too low, too slow to assemble, too flimsy, or too specialized.

Fast

Set up takes seconds, not parts or poles.

Flexible

One table covers standing, seated, and low use cases.

Familiar

The motion is already understood by millions of users.

07 / 10

Outdoor Translation

Keep the mechanism. Rebuild the surface and feet for the field.

The concept should not inherit the weak parts of a household ironing board. It should preserve the deployment logic while correcting the outdoor failure points.

  • Rigid wipeable or perforated hard top
  • Oversized feet for grass, gravel, dirt, and sand
  • Positive lock feedback and pinch-point protection
  • Carry handle, wagon storage, and tie-down logic
08 / 10

Prototype Focus

The next step is not styling. It is proving the mechanism in field conditions.

Stability

Test side loads, uneven ground, and loaded-table adjustment.

Feet

Find a foot geometry that resists sinking without becoming bulky.

Lock

Validate a simple handle/spring lock that feels safe and immediate.

Packout

Confirm folded thickness, carry comfort, and wagon compatibility.

09 / 10

Recommendation

Build the simplest version first.

A Camp / Field Utility Board is the cleanest, most Westfield-ready translation of the ironing-board mechanism. It avoids the novelty trap and focuses on practical outdoor utility.

Prototype the frame, adjustment, lock, feet, and hard top before expanding into any broader product family.
