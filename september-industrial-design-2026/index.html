02 / 30

Instant Indoor / 01

The chair started coming together.

Frame. Suspension. Upholstery.

The telescoping frame removes the awkward split-pole step.

The suspension slats now sit in hidden pockets along the seat and inside arms. They can still be removed for repairs.

We still need the right suspension stiffness, durability testing and a folding check with all the upholstery fitted. The local fiberglass alternative was too soft.

03 / 30

Instant Indoor / 02

The first cover didn’t work.
We had a new one the next day.

The first cover I designed failed.

We replaced the patterns, changed the foam construction, reduced foam weight and revised how the cover attaches at the tops of the arms.

The cutter, sewer and upholsterer were there with me. We could make the decisions and get straight back to work.

From home, I think this would have taken another week or more. Being there meant we could agree on the changes and make them right away.

04 / 30

Instant Indoor / 03

Printed at the office. Fitted that day.

Fuller shape, without adding bulk.

The spacers pushed the lower cover attachments outward, and the first test fit looked good. We also took 20% of the fill out of the back cushion and fixed the problem with inserting and removing the connectors.

Still to do: finish the foam and tailoring, source suspension with the right stiffness, strengthen the arm/back tubes, and check packed size, weight and how the cushions recover.

05 / 30

Tactical Messenger / 01

Parker’s sample gave us a starting point.

We brought the office prototype to the factory.

The first sample missed the shape, materials and how the bag would be used. We needed to improve our specification, too.

Parker’s prototype showed the size and features we wanted. We used Chrome and Freitag bags to explain materials and construction, then worked from Parker’s sample to build our bag.

06 / 30

Tactical Messenger / 02

Four revisions.
Two weeks.

We kept trying it, changing it and trying again.

September 18: simplified the liner, replaced the bulky laptop pocket with a lighter neoprene sleeve, reshaped the flap for strap clearance, and corrected grab loops and webbing.

September 22: a 30-minute loaded walk exposed a sharp body-contact corner and poor flap overlap. The makers wore the bag; we agreed to soften the corner, extend the flap about 5 cm and move reinforcement inside.

07 / 30

Tactical Messenger / 03

Now we can test it with the real gear.

The latest sample is good, but we’re not done.

Indianapolis testing can use firearms, magazines and plates—the loadouts we could not test in China.

Use those loads to check fit, weight distribution, comfort and access. Then take the Indianapolis feedback to the factory for another revision and review in October.

The latest sample worked well. Loading it with the actual gear in Indy will show us what else needs changing.

Flow V2 went off track.

The sample wasn’t built to my design.

The team modified its older model instead of building from my August 4 (“8.4”) CAD.

How we worked / 01

We changed how the next sample got made.

Instead of passing instructions along and waiting for updates, I worked through changes with the people making the samples.

The usual handoff

Send instructions. Wait. Review.

  1. I send CAD or comments.A coordinator passes them to the sample makers.
  2. The sample gets built.Questions and progress come back through the same person.
  3. I review what comes back.If the direction is wrong, we send corrections and build again.

Instant Indoor & Tactical Messenger

Look at it together. Change it. Try it.

  1. Review the actual work with the makers.We can point to the same fit or construction problem.
  2. Agree on the change there and then.I can explain the intent and make the design decision.
  3. Try the revision and keep going.We find the next issue while the sample is still being worked on.

Being in China helped because it changed who I could talk to, what I could see and when we could make decisions.

12 / 30

How we worked / 02

The people making it helped work out the fix.

We didn’t have to send every question back through someone else.

When my first Instant Indoor cover failed, I was there with the cutter, sewer and upholsterer. We could see the problem and work through how to make it differently.

I could make the design calls; they could work out the cutting, sewing and upholstery changes. We had the people we needed to decide and get started.

That meant new patterns, a new foam approach, lighter foam and revised arm-top attachments—not just a small fit adjustment.

The revised cover was ready the next day. I think the same back-and-forth from home would have taken another week or more.

Authority

Know the project and make the call

Recap tutorial / 1 · Set it up

Start with the projects and the people.

I want the details saved with the project and a readable update for Darin. Those are different jobs, but they can use the same notes.

1

Name the projects

Give the agent the project names and the correct Trello card links.

2

Say who needs the update

Specify the recipient and what they need to know: progress, decisions, open issues and next actions.

3

Set the sending rule

For my Darin emails, the agent prepares the full draft and waits for my approval.

Example instruction · adapt for your projects

“Sort my notes, chats and photos by project. Prepare Trello updates for these cards: [links]. Draft one daily recap for [recipient]. Keep missing information marked as unknown, and show me the complete email before sending.”

With access to the relevant board and mailbox, the agent can post or send. Without it, it can still prepare the text.

Recap tutorial / 7 · Review and send

I check the meaning before I approve the email.

A cleanly written draft still needs to say what actually happened. I compare it with the notes, chats and photos—not just check the spelling.

Estimate or finished result?

40 × 40 × 120 cm

This was the packing-trial estimate. It should not become a verified final carton specification.

What is still unknown?

Final weight pending

The agent must keep that open in both updates, not fill in a number or make the work sound complete.

Did the technical meaning survive?

No press-compression of the cover

The installed rigid slats matter. The cover and cushions cannot be described as if they pack the same way.

Correct → regenerate → read back → approve

I send corrections in the same conversation. For Darin, I approve the complete email—including its attachments—before it is sent.

Recap tutorial / 8 · Reuse the record

The daily record made the final recap easier.

The decisions were already saved.

Trello held the project detail. Darin’s daily updates held the narrative. Notes, chats and photos were there when I needed to explain the whole trip.
