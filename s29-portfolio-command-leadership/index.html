When ownership was possession

Whoever had the folder, had the work.

The paper office · one folder, one place, one pair of hands

Its location was the status.

  • The folder traveled desk to desk as the work got done.
  • It could be in exactly one place, in one pair of hands, at a time.
  • So anyone walking the floor could answer: who has it, and what happens next.

Modern tools · everything visible, nothing held

Status became a question.

  • Drives, chat, and boards made everything visible to everyone —
  • — and copies are everywhere at once, so nothing is held.
  • Ownership went from a fact you could see to a question you have to ask.

We are not going back to paper. We are taking back the one thing paper did better: at every moment, one named person has it.

06 / 11

The pull rule

You never throw the folder. The next person takes it.

Setting it on someone’s desk is not a handoff. Neither is moving a card.

1Output and open risks go into the folder.
2The spec travels out; a sample returns. The folder stays in Westfield hands.
3The next owner takes it — one acceptance comment — and a new action and date go on the label.

Why insist on taking? A folder that must be taken can’t be dumped on anyone invisibly. Until the next owner is ready, it waits on the wall — where waiting is visible — not in an inbox, where it isn’t. Factories have run on this rule for seventy years: work moves when the next station pulls it. That is what keeps every queue honest.

HengFeng may build the sample, but a named internal owner holds the folder through the request, the chase, the review, and the acceptance of what comes back.

08 / 11

The clerical staff, without hiring one

Real project management, without a staff of project managers.

The paper office had file clerks and registrars keeping folders moving. The agents are that staff: they remember, remind, brief, and fetch. People decide.

Fig. 7 — The services point, gather, and explain · People retain every decision

The steward

Walks the wall on schedule: flags a blank label field, a missed date, an incomplete gate, a handoff nobody has taken — and keeps advisory clarity questions private until they prove useful.

Personal service

A short morning brief for each participant: the folders you hold, the ones headed your way, what changed — updates only on your explicit request.

Leadership service

The decision queue, milestone risk, and portfolio load — without reconstructing status in the room.

Agents never invent owners, dates, acceptance, approvals, priorities, or business decisions. The gate approval is always typed personally. The board remains fully usable with every agent switched off.

This is why the label stays cheap to maintain: the staff does the remembering; people do the deciding.

The decision dividend

The record pays twice: it runs the work, then informs the decisions.

Tracking is the floor. The reason to keep the folders true is what they can tell us.

On one folder — available immediately

  • Approve development spend with charter, evidence, and risks in one place — a decision, not a document hunt.
  • Park or reopen against explicit written conditions — capital stops dribbling into projects nobody actively chose to continue.

Across the wall — compounding over the season

  • Launch plans built on measured stage durations instead of optimistic dates.
  • Sample-round patterns → sharper specs and better tooling conversations with HengFeng.
  • Load concentration seen before overload escalates.
  • Portfolio mix as a fact, not an impression, via Innovation Type on every label.
