01 / 11

When ownership was possession

Whoever had the folder, had the work.

Then · one physical record

Its location was the status.

  • The folder traveled desk to desk as the work got done.
  • It could be in exactly one place, in one pair of hands, at a time.
  • Anyone walking the floor could see who had it and what happened next.

Now · shared digital access

The work is everywhere; the holder is not.

  • Drives, chat, and boards made the evidence visible to everyone.
  • Copies can be everywhere at once, so nothing is visibly held.
  • Ownership became a question reconstructed from meetings, messages, and memory.
S29 keeps digital accessAt every moment, one named person holds the next move. Everyone else can still see the folder.

The pull rule

You never throw the folder. The next person takes it.

Setting it on someone’s desk is not a handoff. Neither is moving a card.

1 · Pack the resultOutput and open risks go into the folder.
2 · Work travels; accountability staysThe sample goes out and returns. Westfield still holds the folder.
3 · The next owner takes itAcceptance rotates the holder, action, and date.

Fig. 6 — Westfield development · HengFeng sampling · Westfield acceptance

Why pull? Work that must be taken cannot be dumped invisibly. Until the next owner is ready, the folder waits on the wall — where the queue is visible. It is the same pull principle behind kanban.

The external boundary: HengFeng may build the sample, but a named Westfield owner holds the folder through the request, follow-up, review, and acceptance.

The discipline, priced by role

The whole discipline, priced out.

EveryonePlain language on the folder. No tokens, jargon, or new ceremonies.
Current OwnerKeep the five label fields true. Say so, in plain words, when blocked.
Next OwnerTake the folder explicitly — one acceptance comment.
Portfolio Owner / PMOwn the folder through release.
Development approverPersonally record one decision — APPROVED FOR DEVELOPMENT — at one gate.
Leadership — all of usRun reviews from the board. Correct missing status on the folder, not out loud.

The last row decides whether this works. If leadership accepts reconstructed status elsewhere, the board becomes optional for everyone.

08 / 11

The clerical staff, without hiring one

Agents carry the clerical burden. People run the projects.

The paper office had file clerks and registrars keeping folders moving. The agents remember, remind, brief, and fetch. People decide.

The steward

Walks the wall on schedule; flags blank fields, missed dates, incomplete gates, and handoffs nobody has taken. Advisory clarity remains private until separately proven and approved.

Personal service

A short morning brief: folders held, folders arriving, what changed. Updates occur only on that person’s explicit request, under their own identity.

Leadership service

Surfaces the decision queue, milestone risk, and portfolio load without reconstructing status in the room.

Fig. 7 — Cyan is attention, not authority

Agents never invent owners, dates, acceptance, approvals, priorities, or business decisions. The board remains usable with every agent switched off.The approver always types the gate decision personally.

S28 evidence method

What the snapshot measures — and what it does not.

Scope

All 199 open cards across all 19 open Innovation S28 lists, captured Aug. 2, 2026. Strictly read-only.

Assigned member

At least one visible Trello member. A proxy for visible ownership, not proof of the S29 Current Owner contract.

Due date

Trello’s built-in due date populated. S28 does not distinguish an action date from a milestone.

Minimum visible operating record

At least one member, a due date, a non-empty description, and at least one comment. Deliberately less demanding than the S29 contract.

Interpretation boundary

The analysis evaluates S28 as a portfolio operating system — never the performance of a person or function.
