The Project Folder,

S29 — this season’s portfolio board

One product. One folder. One visible holder at every moment.

01 / 11

The design principle

Whoever had the folder, had the work.

Then · visible possession

The folder traveled.

  • It moved desk to desk as the work got done.
  • It carried the brief, drawings, correspondence, and sign-offs.
  • Anyone walking the floor could see who had it and what happened next.

Now · shared visibility

Everything is viewable.

  • Shared drives, chat, and boards made evidence available everywhere.
  • But visibility alone does not say who holds the next move.
  • Ownership became a question people reconstruct instead of a fact they can see.

We are not going back to paper. We are taking back the one thing paper did better.

The master record

One persistent folder per product — from idea to launch.

The folder moves. The history stays together.

  • Brief, concept evidence, specifications, samples, tests, decisions, and retrospectives accumulate in one record.
  • Execution teams keep their detailed boards and tools; links point downward without creating a second authoritative record.
  • Parked and cancelled folders retain the learning.

The accountable handoff

A handoff is complete when the next person takes the folder.

Setting it on someone’s desk is not a handoff. Neither is moving a card.

Fig. 7 — Westfield development → HengFeng sampling → Westfield review

1Output and open risks go into the folder.
2The Next Owner explicitly accepts — one comment, in their own words.
3Possession rotates; a new action and date go on the label.

HengFeng may build the sample, but a named internal owner holds the folder through the request, follow-up, review, and acceptance of what comes back.

07 / 11

The human discipline

The whole discipline, priced out.

EveryoneUse plain language on the folder. No tokens, jargon, or new ceremonies.
Portfolio Owner / PMOwn the product identity, overall accountability, target milestone, and intended path through release.
Current OwnerKeep the current result, date, output, open risks, and Blocked Reason true.
Next OwnerTake the folder explicitly — one acceptance comment.
Development approverPersonally record one decision — APPROVED FOR DEVELOPMENT — at one gate.
Leadership — all of usRun reviews from the board. Correct missing status on the folder, not out loud.

The last row is the one that decides whether this works.

08 / 11

The agentic layer

Real portfolio discipline, without adding a project-management layer.

The paper office had file clerks and registrars keeping folders moving. The agents absorb that routing and memory burden. People decide.

Fig. 8 — One shelf, three services · The services point and explain; people retain every decision

The steward

Walks the shelf on schedule, flags objective gaps and missed commitments, and keeps advisory clarity questions private until they prove useful.

Personal service

Gives each participant a short brief: folders held, folders arriving, what changed — and updates only on explicit request.

Leadership service

Surfaces the decision queue, milestone risk, and portfolio load without reconstructing status in the room.

Agents never invent owners, dates, acceptance, approvals, priorities, or business decisions. The board remains fully usable with every agent switched off.

This season’s rollout

S29 arrives the way every season’s board does — improved by the last one.

The sequence

An initial set of live projects loads first, with their owners — not a migration of everything at once.
Personal morning briefs switch on at activation, so people feel the payback first.
Objective steward exception comments follow once board use settles. Advisory clarity remains private until separately proven and approved.

Run reviews from the board. If the folder is wrong, fix the folder.
