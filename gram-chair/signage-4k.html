02 — The idea

The frame is the mechanism.

Eight elastomeric corners replace the rigid elbows. Under a weight shift the side profile shears square to parallelogram — that geometry change is the rocking arc, and the strain stored in the corners puts the chair back.

8 corners · twin Ø22.7 mm tube interface · moulded stops

04 — How it rocks

Nothing brings you back but the corners.

All eight corners flex through the same angle, together. Lean and gravity keeps pulling you further over — nothing in the geometry brings you back. Every bit of the return comes from the corners themselves. Get the corner right and the chair rocks. That is the whole design.

05 — Tuned to order

You pick how it feels.

06 — The window

There is a narrow band where a chair rocks.

Too soft and it sags under you. Too stiff and you have a very well-built chair that will not move. The band is 120–300 N·m/rad, and it is the shape of the corner that puts you inside it — a softer rubber alone will not. The recommended corner sits at 240.

Hitting the band is the design problem · everything else is the frame you already build

07 — It keeps rocking

It rocks, it centres, and it keeps doing it.

A 250 lb rider gets 2.6× more push back than it takes to bring the chair level — and lighter riders get more, not less. At full lean the rubber is working at 14% strain against a 50% limit, and 8% against 20% on a normal rock. Sag under load stays under 3 mm.
