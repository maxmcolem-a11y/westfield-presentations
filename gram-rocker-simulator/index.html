What the model is
  • One rule makes the whole thing work: the skids stay planted and the frame shears square→parallelogram, so all 8 corners flex together by the lean angle θ. The corners resist with 8·k·θ; gravity pushes the rider further over with m·g·L·sinθ (L = 655 mm leg span). Corner strength must beat gravity — that's the red zone on the slider, and it moves as you change rider weight.
  • Too strong is also a failure: past the green band the rider's weight shift can't command a useful arc — the chair is stable but dead. The as-designed CAD corners in TPU 95A measure 1,324–1,756 N·m/rad (marked on the slider).
  • Print recipe: your samples are 3-D printed, so infill is the tuning knob. The readout assumes the recommended corner geometry (3 mm wall / 25 mm flexure) printed solid in TPU 95A = 240 N·m/rad, and scales stiffness with infill by a power law (k ∝ infill^1.8, typical for gyroid/grid). Uncalibrated — print one corner, twist-test it, and I'll fit the real exponent.
  • Damping is a typical elastomer loss factor (ζ ≈ 0.15). Stops are stiff bumpers at the travel angle. Corner stiffness ↔ geometry/material comes from the FEA side (gramcorner CLI in 06_corner_design_tool/).
  • 2-D single-degree-of-freedom idealization: no side-load/racking, fold, or cushion compliance. Strain and fatigue checks live in the CLI reports.
