How we'll judge the sample

These are outcomes, not methods. If the sample does all of these, the build is right, whatever it looks like inside.

TestTarget
One pull from flat, on grassStands up with no other help. If it tips onto its side, setting it upright is fine.
Size★ About 1.6 m tall, 2.5 m between the feet
Pull forceComfortable for one adult. We'll measure it; the target is that the last part of the pull stays under roughly 13 kg.
Locked upEvery joint fully closed. The catch holds for an hour with no slipping.
ReleaseOne flick, and every joint springs open by itself.
PackStuffs into a sack about the length of one pole section (≈ 50 cm).
Repeat20× up and down: no joint stuck open or shut, no worn cord, nothing loose.
03

The parts: what each has to do

Where everything sits

Pole sections

Fibreglass tube, like a normal tent pole: about Ø11 mm, in sections about ★ 500 mm long. Use your nearest stock tube and tell us what it is; we'll check the stiffness.

Joints: the important part

All 28 joints are the same. Each one has to do three things:

Corners (×4)

Arch tops (×2)

Cords and the pull

Doorway straps (×2)

04

Fabric and sewing

Skin: one rectangle of shade fabric, roughly 4.5 × 1.5 m flat. Use a light UPF polyester you already use for shade products. It should be the right size on the frame when the frame is closed.

★ Sleeves with slack. When the cord is released, every joint springs open and the poles get several centimetres longer. The sleeve only needs enough slack, around and along the pole, that it never grips the pole and stops a joint springing fully open and going loose. Leave the sleeves open at the corners, and don't tie the fabric corners tight to the corner pieces.
  • A sleeve runs across the middle for the ridge pole, with an opening at each arch top for the T.
  • Along one side pole, a small channel on top of the sleeve carries the cords to the catch.
  • Doorway straps are attached only at their ends, so they slide freely.
05

Short requests for outside suppliers

If a part has to come from outside, send only the relevant line, never this brief, and no photos of the sample.

Tube: fibreglass tube, about Ø11 mm outside with a bore of about Ø7 mm, cut to 500 mm. Please send stock sizes and stiffness data.
Metal sleeves: aluminium tube, about Ø14 mm outside, a sliding fit over Ø11 mm tube, about 120 mm long, one end flared. Please send samples and say how the flare is formed.
Foam inserts: a small sleeve of firm foam, about Ø10 mm, giving 2–3 kg when squeezed from 60 to 30 mm long. Please send a few firmness options.
Catch: a one-way cleat or cam for 3 mm Dyneema line, with flick release, holding about 15 kg. Please send options.
06

Suggested order

  1. Joint trial: about 10 joints in 2–3 variations (e.g. two foam firmnesses and two mouth shapes). Check close, open and re-entry by hand.
  2. One arch: 9 sections in a loose sleeve with a doorway strap. Lay it flat, pull, check the height, release, stuff, and repeat.
  3. Full sample, once 1 and 2 work.
At each step, send us photos and video internally, with what you changed and why. Those changes are the most useful thing the sample gives us.
